01 / Definition

Complexity through transformation.

Instead of describing a model only by parameter count, depth or runtime, the framework asks how its internal and behavioral states transform—and which properties remain stable.

Lie Computability is a proposed approach to computation in which continuous transformations, their infinitesimal generators and invariant structure provide a language for characterizing what an intelligent system can represent, change and preserve.Working definition for this research program. “Lie” refers to Sophus Lie’s mathematical theory of continuous symmetry; this proposal should not be confused with the classical theorem about decidability of Lie algebras.
02 / Model complexity

Beyond counting parameters.

A model may be large yet behaviorally simple, or compact yet dynamically difficult to predict. Lie Computability reframes complexity as geometry plus reachable transformation.

03 / Oversight

From latent dynamics to reviewable evidence.

The oversight aim is to constrain and audit model change—not merely score final outputs.

01

Observe

Collect activations, trajectories, tool calls, policy shifts and human feedback.

02

Decompose

03

Measure whether claimed invariants survive perturbation, adaptation and deployment shift.

04

Govern

Gate actions when transformations leave an approved region or invalidate an assurance claim.

05 / Mathematical core

Measurements tied to structure.

The implementation combines tensor topology with non-commutative geometry. These are model-analysis constructs; their value for safety must be established empirically.

Lie Computability of Lie Lattices of Tensor based topologies of networks

The paper studies the computational complexity of tensor-based network architectures through Lie lattice theory. It considers embeddings in exceptional and classical algebraic structures—including E8 and D4—alongside asymptotic behavior and possible consequences for supervised and unsupervised learning.

Its research vocabulary connects Lie algebras and tensor decomposition with complexity classes and notions including Z-completeness, ZPP, BPP, P, NP and QP. These connections form a proposed theoretical program and should be read as research claims to be formalized and tested.

08 / Research position

A proposal, with a path to proof.

Lie Computability is presented here as Dr. Bheemaiah Anil Kumar’s coined research framework—not as an established consensus field. Its value depends on definitions, theorems and experiments that outperform simpler baselines.

What it could enable

Symmetry-aware complexity measures; auditable state-transition boundaries; invariant-based safety cases; and lower-dimensional descriptions of agent behavior.

What must be demonstrated

A precise computability model, links between algebraic structure and real failure modes, tractable estimators, benchmark comparisons, and limits or counterexamples.

Alignment connection

Alignment asks whether system behavior remains compatible with intended objectives and constraints. Invariants can encode candidate properties; transformation analysis can expose how training or adaptation threatens them.

Oversight connection

Oversight needs evidence a reviewer can inspect. Generator-level traces and invariant tests could create intermediate evidence between raw internals and a model’s final answer.
